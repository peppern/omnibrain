## Main Point

Gresham says an AI-agent swarm can be made far cheaper by cutting wasted work (oversized inputs, repeated research, empty checks and runaway retries), and lists 20 open-source tools plus a long course on how to do it.

## Takeaways

- The headline claim (a 17-year-old runs 300 bots for under $0.02 per bot a week, versus $4,500 a week before) is the author's own and isn't backed by any evidence in the post.
- The 20 repos fall into a few jobs: budget and route model calls (LiteLLM, RouteLLM, Semantic Router, Portkey), cache and reuse results (GPTCache, requests-cache, Hishel, Prefect), shrink or select inputs (Crawl4AI, Firecrawl, Chonkie, FlagEmbedding, LLMLingua, RTK), memory (Mem0, Graphiti), scheduling and queues (Celery, APScheduler), stopping failure loops (PyBreaker) and tracing (Phoenix).
- The quoted course works through one example, a daily competitor-pricing brief: audit the waste first, hand off through short BRIEF.md/HANDOFF.md files instead of long chats, extract only the passages a question needs, and check for changes before running a full analysis.
- It also says to give saved results a refresh rule so they're reused rather than redone, give each task one owner, allow at most two attempts on a failing step and save progress first, and define 'done' as a checklist with PASS/FAIL/UNKNOWN.
- Its '7 to 70 usable results on the same budget' math (100,000 to 12,500 tokens per assignment, 70% to 87.5% acceptance) is a worked illustration, not a measured result; it says to compare workflows on the same inputs and checklist.
- The 24-second video is a silent infographic of the same 20 repos, so there's nothing to transcribe.
