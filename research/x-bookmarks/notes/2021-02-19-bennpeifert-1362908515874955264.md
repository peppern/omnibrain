## Main Point

Benn Eifert's thread argues that tail hedges lose money on their own yet can raise a rebalanced portfolio's long-term compound return.

## Takeaways

- Volatility drag: two streams that both average 10% compound at 9.5% vs 8.0% when their volatility is 10% vs 20% (the drag is about half the variance).
- It's true that tail hedges should have negative expected returns over time, or markets would be wildly inefficient.
- But hedges pay off big in crashes, so a regularly rebalanced portfolio can buy more risk assets right after drawdowns.
- He backtests a plain strategy (1-year 30-delta puts, delta-hedged, rolled quarterly) added to a 60/40 portfolio and shows higher long-term returns, not just lower volatility.
