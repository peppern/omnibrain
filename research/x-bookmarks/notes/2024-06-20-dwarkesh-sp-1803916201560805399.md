## Main Point

Dwarkesh Patel argues smart, ambitious people should treat a high-quality blog or podcast as a real career.

## Takeaways

- He almost quit his podcast many times and wishes he'd treated it as his main thing earlier.
- Most will fail, but we don't discourage founders for the same reason; the financial upside is higher than people expect.
- You also get a dream job: reading, writing and talking to interesting people.
- He suggests giving it a serious full-time 3 months (the length of YC); this came from his appearance on Chris Best's Substack interview.

## Original Post

> We talked about why smart, ambitious people should consider producing a high quality blog/podcast/etc as a real career.  
> 
> I started the podcast on a whim and almost quit to go do something "real" so many times over the year. 
> 
> I wish I had taken the podcast more seriously as "the main thing I'm doing and will eventually make money from".
> 
> Of course you'll probably fail. But we don't tell startup founders that they're doing something dumb because of the same logic! If they succeed, they'll produce something really valuable for the world, and also make a lot of money.
> 
> You won't make IPOed Facebook money. But if I could convey one thing to my younger self who almost quit, it's that the upside (even just in financial terms) is way higher than you might anticipate. 
> 
> And the non-financial benefit is that you get to have a dream job where you read and write and talk to interesting people for a living! 
> 
> Can't you spare 3 months full time (the length of Y-combinator) to take a serious shot at making your dream job real?
> 
> Yes, I know that me saying all this reeks of selection effects. But you're smart enough to factor that into any decisions you might make.
> 
> Who better to chat about this with than the CEO of Substack, which is making careers like this not only possible but lucrative! Thanks so much for the great conversation Chris!
