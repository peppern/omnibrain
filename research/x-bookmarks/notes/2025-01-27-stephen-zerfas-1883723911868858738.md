## Main Point

Stephen Zerfas praises Nick Cammarata's take on common misconceptions in meditation language.

## Takeaways

- Cammarata's 'blackest pill' was that 'all sensations are suffering'; he now thinks that's false or misinterpreted.
- Zerfas: 'thinning things out' into high resolution and sensitivity feels extremely good and close to love.
- Getting 'closer to where sensations come from' is a useful modern pointer.
- Better phrasing: if things are thinned out you suffer less, and the upper bound of bliss and peace is near-unbelievable.

## Original Post

> Terrifically lucid take on both misconceptions of common meditation language, and meditation "secrets" (i.e. few people believe to be true but they turn out to be true and extremely valuable).
> 
> - "Thinning things out" into high resolution, sensitivity, and conductivity feels almost unbelievably good. And it ends up being so "love" adjacent that it makes sense some enlightenment systems are basically just giving love all the way there.
> 
> - "Getting closer to where sensations come from and away 'from you'" is a great pointer in modern language.
> 
> - "all sensation is suffering" may be terribly misleading and better said "if things are thinned out, you'll experience less bliss-peace, and the upper bound is borderline unbelievable"
