## Main Point

Jason Liu argues RAG and search systems feel broken because they treat everything, including numbers, places and categories, as text strings.

## Takeaways

- Most RAG systems are really recommendation systems in disguise.
- A query like 'popular family-friendly hotels with good wi-fi near midtown under $400' mixes numeric, categorical, semantic, location and hard-filter signals.
- Filters crudely approximate smooth preferences, re-ranking is a hack, and text embeddings don't understand numbers.
- Fix: a mix of specialized encoders for each data type, plus your behavioral data; he cites 50% engagement lifts.

## Original Post

> why your rag system feels broken: it's seeing the world as strings
> 
> i've been saying this for a while: most rag systems are just recommendation systems in disguise. but there's a fundamental problem with how we're building them.
> 
> your search pilot sees the entire world as strings.
> 
> this hit me during daniel's talk yesterday. when users type "popular family-friendly hotels with good wi-fi near manhattan midtown under $400," they're expressing multiple types of signals:
> numerical bias (popularity)
> categorical bias (family-friendly)
> semantic bias (good wi-fi)
> location bias (near manhattan)
> hard filter (under $400)
> 
> but if your toolkit is just text embedding models, you're screwed from the start.
> 
> the three broken pieces of modern search:
> filters are overused to approximate biases, using crude step functions when user preferences are actually smooth sigmoid curves
> 
> re-ranking is a hack that only works on <1% of your data. nobody would need it if the underlying retrieval worked properly
> 
> text embeddings don't understand non-text data. stringifying numbers and feeding them to text models creates unpredictable, noisy results
> 
> the solution? a mixture of specialized encoders that actually understand different data types.
> 
> instead of forcing everything through text models, we need encoders that natively handle location data, numerical values, categorical attributes, and interaction graphs, then combine their outputs intelligently.
> 
> this isn't just theoretical. when you implement this properly, you get 50% lifts in engagement metrics. the signal is in your behavioral data. often 100tb of clicks sitting unused while your product catalog is barely 100gb.
> 
> your llm can't save you here. no amount of re-ranking or prompt engineering fixes the fundamental issue: your pilot sees numbers as strings, not values.
> 
> use the right tools for the job. your users will thank you.
