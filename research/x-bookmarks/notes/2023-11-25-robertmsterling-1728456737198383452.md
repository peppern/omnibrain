## Original Post

> Under-discussed and semi-controversial topic in wealth management: High-income parents who can provide their children luxurious lifestyles through college but who don’t have wealth sufficient to provide financial support into adult years.
> 
> These kids grow up with extremely high quality of life but are then shocked at how difficult it becomes to maintain that same lifestyle once they have to fund it on their own. And it can lead to some really poor decision-making, often with regard to credit-card spending. Especially when they see friends of theirs on Instagram who *do* have trust funds and who *haven’t* had to alter their lifestyles once they’re on their own.
> 
> If you’re a parent in this situation, ask yourself if you’re setting the lifestyle bar too high for your kids and creating standards that won’t be realistic for them once the direct deposits start coming from an entry-level job, rather than from your own checking account. Ask yourself if it’s worth raising teenagers accustomed to driving Acuras when they’ll probably be buying used Hondas once they’re the ones making the payments. Ask yourself if you want a future 25-year-old son disappointed that his vacations won’t be at the Four Seasons for a while.
> 
> If this is you—and, candidly, it probably is me—it might be worth dialing back on your own lifestyle. Not only will you set standards that are more attainable for your kids, you’ll also be able to set more wealth aside so that you will be able to support your kids, if you so choose to. And talk to your kids about all this—be transparent about your financial position, educate them about the value of proper stewardship of resources, and show them that there are things in life far more valuable than status and consumption.
