## Main Point

Bryan Johnson's thread (recovered in full) argues seed oils' bad reputation is unfounded; the harms come from how they're cooked and from low omega-3 intake.

## Takeaways

- He cites large cohorts (500k US adults, 14k Chinese adults) where swapping animal fat for plant oil was linked to lower mortality, plus meta-analyses showing no rise in inflammation, diabetes or cancer risk.
- The real problems are deep frying, overheating and reheating oils (oxidized linoleic acid produces toxic byproducts), and an omega-6:3 ratio near 15:1 versus an ideal of about 2–3:1.
- Palm oil's tentative cancer signal (palmitic acid in rodent studies) doesn't apply to unsaturated seed oils.
- Practical advice: add oils after cooking, stir-fry at moderate heat, and eat more omega-3 (he takes 1.5 g of algal omega-3 a day). These are his claims with his citations, not independently checked.
