## Claim

AI buildout demand for power and materials could make energy and commodities such as copper a simple, liquid and leverageable way to get AI exposure that investors are overlooking. _(Noah's thesis)_

## Drivers and catalysts

- data-center power demand
- copper demand for electrification and grid build-out

## Risks

- AI capex slows or is already priced into energy and copper
- commodity prices are driven mostly by factors unrelated to AI

## Kill criteria (what would prove it wrong)

- AI-related demand turns out to be a small share of power or copper demand growth

### Agent interpretation

_Agent inference, not owner framing:_ The thesis splits in two. The US power leg is real: data centres are nearly half of US electricity demand growth to 2030 (IEA) and listed power producers hold direct AI offtake contracts, and power equipment is supply-constrained. But it is not overlooked: those stocks fell more than 20% on the DeepSeek day and trade as high-beta AI proxies. The copper leg is the reverse: liquid, leverageable and not crowded, but AI is only about 2% of 2030 copper demand and ICSG sees refined surpluses through 2027, so copper is mostly not AI exposure. As framed (simple, overlooked AI exposure via energy and copper) the claim is weak; a narrower thesis on supply-constrained US power and grid equipment is better supported but already priced as AI beta.
