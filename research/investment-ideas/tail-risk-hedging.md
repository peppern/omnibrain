# Tail-risk hedging and variance drag

> **Not investment advice.** Dossiers summarize what the cited sources claim, with verbatim evidence; stances are the sources' own. Nothing here is a recommendation.

Theme: `tail-risk-hedging` · Sources: 1 · Published 2021-02-19 → 2021-02-19 · First seen by pipeline: 2026-10-16

Portfolio-level effects of volatility, drawdowns and option-based tail hedges.

## Theses and stances

- **contrarian** · framework · [@bennpeifert, 2021-02-19](https://x.com/bennpeifert/status/1362908515874955264): Tail hedges are expected to lose money on their own, yet adding them to a regularly rebalanced portfolio can raise its long-term compound return by cushioning drawdowns. — “they can enhance the long-term compound rate of return of the overall investment program” (main) / “despite the hedges themselves losing money over long periods of time on a stand-alone basis” (main)

## Catalysts

- Volatility itself lowers compound growth (variance drag), so reducing drawdowns has value. — “volatility itself reduces the long-term compound rate of growth of a portfolio, via a phenomenon called variance drag” (main) [@bennpeifert, 2021-02-19](https://x.com/bennpeifert/status/1362908515874955264)

## Risks

- Tail hedges should not be expected to make money systematically over time; carry is a real cost. — “it is unrealistically optimistic to think that tail risk hedges can make money systematically over time” (main) [@bennpeifert, 2021-02-19](https://x.com/bennpeifert/status/1362908515874955264)
